### Boehm’s Software Quality Model — Discussion

**Student:** What is Boehm’s Software Quality Model?  
**Teacher:** It is a hierarchical software quality model introduced by B.W. Boehm in 1978. It evaluates software quality using multiple levels instead of a single flat list.

**Student:** What makes it different from other models?  
**Teacher:** It is broader than models like McCall’s. It includes more characteristics and even considers hardware-related performance aspects.

**Student:** What are the levels in this model?  
**Teacher:** There are three levels:
1. Primary Uses  
2. Intermediate Constructs  
3. Primitive Constructs  

**Student:** What are the primary uses?  
**Teacher:** The model defines three main goals:
- As-is Utility → How usable the software is in its current form  
- Maintainability → Effort required to find and fix defects  
- Portability → Effort required to move software to another environment  

**Student:** What are the quality factors?  
**Teacher:** The model includes:
- Portability  
- Reliability  
- Efficiency  
- Usability (Human Engineering)  
- Testability  
- Understandability  
- Modifiability  

**Student:** What are primitive constructs?  
**Teacher:** These are detailed measurable attributes like:
- Accuracy, Completeness, Consistency  
- Device Efficiency, Device Independence  
- Accessibility, Communicativeness  
- Self-descriptiveness, Structuredness, Conciseness  

**Student:** Example?  
**Teacher:** Testability can be broken into:
- Accessibility  
- Communicativeness  
- Structuredness  
- Self-descriptiveness  

**Student:** Final summary?  
**Teacher:** Boehm’s model breaks software quality into layers, from high-level goals to detailed measurable attributes, making evaluation more structured and complete.
